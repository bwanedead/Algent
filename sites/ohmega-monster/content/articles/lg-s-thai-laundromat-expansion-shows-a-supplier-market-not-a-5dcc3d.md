Reports citing LG Electronics, the South Korean appliance maker expanding into business-to-business laundry equipment, say it has supplied commercial washers and dryers to 630 outlets operated by Thailand’s Trendy Wash and expects roughly 400 additional stores in the pipeline. The figures show LG pursuing a substantial Thai customer relationship; they do not prove that LG has 1,000 operating outlets, that Thailand’s laundromat market is growing at the same rate, or that the Philippines has a comparable network buildout.

## What LG says it is building

Commercial-laundry appliances are heavy-duty washers, dryers and related systems sold to businesses such as laundromats rather than household users. LG has made that business a stated growth area, combining machines with localized operating models and LaundryCrew, a platform that lets operators monitor equipment status, operating data and settings remotely.

The reported Thai relationship is with Trendy Wash, described in the coverage as Thailand’s second-largest laundromat franchise. BusinessKorea said LG had completed supply to all 630 Trendy Wash stores and had confirmed further deliveries to about 400 stores the chain planned to open during the year. Seoul Economic Daily described the prospective total as more than 1,000 locations.

Those numbers remain LG-related reports rather than independently confirmed outlet data. The first-party LG commercial-laundry announcement reviewed for this story describes the company’s products, software and a Laundry Crew franchise model in Thailand, but does not name Trendy Wash, confirm the 630 installations or define what the approximately 400 planned outlets represent. It also gives no absolute sales figures or accounting basis for LG’s reported growth rates.

LG says its commercial-laundry sales in Southeast Asia rose about 60% year on year in the prior year, while sales in Thailand more than doubled and sales in the Philippines rose by more than 50%. Those are supplier sales increases from an undisclosed base, not measurements of total laundromat demand. The reports also do not establish whether “supplied” means installed, contracted, operating or merely delivered equipment.

The figures should not be combined with the roughly 600 locations separately reported for WashXpress, a different Thai laundromat chain. Adding the two would mix separate companies, dates and definitions rather than produce a national total.

## Thailand already had a specialist route

LG’s move is entering an existing commercial-laundry supplier field: appliance makers, specialist equipment companies, distributors, franchise builders and service providers that equip and maintain laundromats.

Alliance Laundry Systems, the United States commercial-laundry equipment company whose Speed Queen brand is used by WashXpress, described a Thai operation that predates LG’s current announcement. Alliance said it entered Thailand in 2017 with six laundromats using its equipment, then expanded through distributors, franchise-investment events, service centres and a manufacturing and research facility in the country.

Alliance reported that 1,565 laundromats used its products and services in 2021 and projected 2,565 for 2022. It also claimed almost 65% of Thailand’s laundromat market in 2022 and forecast an 80% share by 2025. Those figures came from Alliance and have not been independently measured in the available evidence. They show how the specialist supplier presents its position, not an audited market ranking.

The commercial model Alliance describes is different from LG’s named-account story. Alliance says distributors act as commercial partners and franchise builders, often handling installation, maintenance and expansion under established laundromat brands. In practice, supplier competition can turn on spare parts, service response, machine sizing, installation and operating costs as much as on the appliance brand itself.

Samsung and Electrolux also market commercial or self-service laundry products. Their regional product materials, however, do not establish Thai contracts, distributors, customers or installed bases. The public evidence therefore cannot support a market-share contest among LG, Alliance/Speed Queen, Samsung and Electrolux.

## The Philippines is a thinner, distributor-led case

LG’s Philippine expansion is described through three named organizations that distribute, install or support its equipment—ELS, Big Wash and Express Clean—rather than an equivalent announcement of equipment supplied across a named nationwide franchise. The available material does not establish those organizations’ outlet totals, ownership, geographic coverage or installed bases.

Alliance presents a similar but separately described route. Its executives call the Philippines less mature than Thailand, with smaller laundry setups potentially moving toward larger, standardized laundromats. Alliance says it reaches such markets through distributors that can sell equipment, install and maintain it, and help build franchises.

That is a plausible commercial explanation for why the Philippines may offer an earlier-stage opportunity. It is not evidence of a measured national buildout. Nor does LG’s reported Philippine sales increase establish category-wide growth.

## What the evidence still cannot measure

Hot, humid weather, urbanization, rental housing, tourism and more single-person households could all support pay-per-use laundry. The available reporting does not show which factor matters most, how many households own washing machines, or whether laundromats are replacing home washing or supplementing it.

The strongest conclusion is narrower: LG is investing in a business-to-business commercial-laundry strategy, and Thailand provides a visible example of supplier expansion through a large reported franchise relationship. Alliance’s earlier Thai network shows that LG is entering an existing specialist ecosystem rather than creating the field from scratch. The Philippines shows a less directly documented, distributor-led path.

No independent national series was found for laundromat outlets, commercial-washer shipments, laundromat revenue or supplier market share in either country. Until those measures—or confirmations from Trendy Wash and the Philippine distributors—become available, the reported deployments are evidence of supplier opportunity, not proof of a Southeast Asian laundromat boom.
