Reuters supports one clear part of the picture: the United States was adjusting its force posture in the region and reinforcing the existing fleet of aerial refueling aircraft stationed in Israel with additional refueling aircraft as attacks by the United States and Iran escalated in the recent U.S.-Iran clashes and strikes. Tanker planes top up other aircraft in flight, which lets U.S. or partner aircraft stay on station farther from base or reach targets without coming back to refuel. Reuters also reported that, as of June 25, there were 98 U.S. military planes in Israel, and that some refueling aircraft were stationed at Israeli Air Force bases and commercial airports for operational and logistical reasons.

The more dramatic claim comes from OSINTtechnical, which posted that the “full-scale US airbridge to the Middle East has returned” — meaning a sustained cargo-and-transport flight pattern linking Europe staging bases to the Middle East — and that “dozens of USAF transport flights hopped from key bases in Europe to the Middle East today,” citing Flightradar24. The post itself does not provide a readable flight log, route list, aircraft list, or destination list.

Reuters gives context, but it does not bridge that gap. In a separate report on the Iran strikes, Reuters said U.S. forces used fighter aircraft, aerial drones and warships, and that strikes hit surveillance sites, military logistics infrastructure, underground weapons storage and maritime capabilities. Reuters also did not mention any Europe-to-Middle East transport surge or increase in airlift flights.

That leaves the route claim unproven here. The exact departure bases, destination bases, aircraft types, and whether the movement was outside normal tanker or airlift variability are not established in this pass. A routine explanation remains plausible: Reuters said the U.S. had already stationed dozens of refueling aircraft in Israel and had shifted some aircraft to Israeli Air Force bases and commercial airports for operational and logistical reasons, which leaves room for rotation, maintenance, crew cycling, basing management, or theater resupply rather than a discrete, proven Europe-to-Middle-East surge.

So the honest reading is split. There is a verified posture change in Israel and an unverified allegation of a broader Europe-to-Middle-East transport spike. Watch it closely, but do not treat the surge as established until independent flight-track evidence or a baseline comparison appears.
