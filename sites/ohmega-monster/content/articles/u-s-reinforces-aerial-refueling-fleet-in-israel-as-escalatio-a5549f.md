There are two pictures in the open record, and they do not fully line up.

**What independent reporting supports.** Reuters reported that the United States was adjusting its force posture in the region and reinforcing the existing fleet of aerial refueling aircraft stationed in Israel with additional tankers as U.S.-Iran attacks escalated. Tanker planes top up other aircraft in flight, which lets U.S. or partner aircraft stay on station farther from base or reach targets without returning to refuel. Reuters also reported that, as of June 25, there were 98 U.S. military planes in Israel, and that some refueling aircraft were stationed at Israeli Air Force bases and commercial airports for operational and logistical reasons.

In a separate report on Iran strikes, Reuters said U.S. forces used fighter aircraft, aerial drones and warships, and that strikes hit surveillance sites, military logistics infrastructure, underground weapons storage and maritime capabilities. That is a picture of intensified in-theater activity — but it is not a Europe-to-Middle-East transport log.

**What people on X said they were noticing.** On the same beat, open-source chatter on X was pointing at air traffic, not just basing. [In a post on X](https://x.com/Osinttechnical/status/2080393908685566041), the public account @Osinttechnical — an OSINT-style account, not a wire or official organ — claimed the “full-scale US airbridge to the Middle East has returned,” meaning a sustained cargo-and-transport pattern from Europe staging bases into the Middle East, and that “dozens of USAF transport flights hopped from key bases in Europe to the Middle East today,” citing Flightradar24 screenshots. That is a speech-act we can check (the account did post it). It is **not** independent confirmation that the flights happened as described.

[Post on X · @Osinttechnical](https://x.com/Osinttechnical/status/2080393908685566041)

The post does not provide a readable flight log, route list, aircraft list, or destination list. Reuters does not mention any Europe-to-Middle East transport surge or increase in airlift flights in the pieces we read. So the tension is the story: **wire-verified tanker reinforcement in Israel** on one side; **X pulse claiming a broader airbridge** on the other.

A routine explanation remains plausible for some of the motion: Reuters said the U.S. had already stationed dozens of refueling aircraft in Israel and had shifted some aircraft to Israeli Air Force bases and commercial airports, which leaves room for rotation, maintenance, crew cycling, basing management, or theater resupply rather than a discrete, proven Europe-to-Middle-East surge.

So the honest reading is split. Treat the posture change in Israel as the grounded logistics fact. Treat the Europe-to-Middle-East airbridge claim as early social sensing — the kind of pulse X often carries before wires digest flight-level detail — not as established logistics fact. Watch it; do not launder it into certainty until independent flight-track evidence or a baseline comparison appears.
