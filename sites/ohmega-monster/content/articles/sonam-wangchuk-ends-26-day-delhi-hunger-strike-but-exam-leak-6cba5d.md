Sonam Wangchuk ended a 26-day hunger strike in New Delhi, but the story did not end there. The protest he had helped amplify was rooted in India’s exam-fairness fight: alleged paper leaks, especially around NEET, India’s national medical entrance exam, and demands for accountability, reform and the resignation of Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`, `[`[ent_1e9b5b6bf5](#)`

That first fact matters because it sets the frame. Wangchuk’s fast was not the movement itself; it was one pressure tactic inside a wider campaign. Reuters said the Cockroach Janta Party, a group associated with the protest movement, had supporters still camping in Delhi and said they would remain until their demands were met, while the BBC said protesters continued at Jantar Mantar even after Wangchuk stopped fasting. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

So why did he stop? The best-supported answer is that he did so after negotiations and because he feared the situation could turn more violent. Reuters described the end of the fast as a negotiated move taken in view of possible violence, and BBC reporting adds that Wangchuk said he had assurances that no legal action would be taken against protesters at Jantar Mantar or those who marched to Parliament. Parliament is India’s national legislature, and in this dispute the promise was that lawmakers would take up the leak, accountability and reform issues there. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

But the bargain was limited. Wangchuk said he had also been assured that the alleged NEET paper leak, accountability in the examination system and wider exam reform would be discussed in Parliament. He said he had received no specific promise that Pradhan would resign, and the gathered sources do not establish whether the assurances were written or merely verbal. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

That is the core reason this is a phase change, not closure. The resignation demand stayed alive, the coalition stayed mobilized, and the hunger strike’s end was best read as a tactical pause rather than a settlement of the underlying dispute. Reuters said the movement would continue protesting, and its supporters were still demanding Pradhan’s exit. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

The government, meanwhile, mixed remedial signals with coercive crowd control. Reuters reported that police used tear gas and batons to disperse marchers trying to reach Parliament, while Delhi Police described the protesters as unruly, aggressive and violent and said they had violated prohibitory orders — orders restricting gatherings or march routes issued by authorities. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

The crowd-control side was not limited to the street. The Ministry of Home Affairs ordered mobile internet suspended for eight hours around Jantar Mantar, and the official order says it was justified by public safety, public order and the prevention of incitement. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

At the same time, Delhi and the federal government were also signaling action on the underlying grievance. Reuters said Modi told lawmakers the government had acted on exam irregularities, 13 people had been arrested and jailed, and a re-examination had been successfully conducted. Reuters and the BBC also reported promises of tougher anti-leak legislation; the BBC said a draft would go to cabinet and then Parliament. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

That does not settle the controversy, though. The NTA — the National Testing Agency, which runs NEET — said before the exam that NEET (UG) 2026 was secure and explicitly warned candidates not to believe rumors or fraudulent claims of paper leaks. But Reuters reported that the May exam was later cancelled after papers were found to have been leaked, that a retest followed, and that the issue had already triggered major student anger. The result is a disputed record, not a cleanly closed one. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

The policing dispute is similar: the state says it was maintaining order, while other reporting shows harm. The Hindu reported that the Supreme Court declined to urgently take up allegations of police excesses during the crackdown and refused to examine videos, but it also said at least one injured protester had pellet-gun injuries. That is not a merits ruling on police conduct; it is evidence that the dispersal was not simply routine or harmless. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

Put plainly, the only clearly supported “settlement” was Wangchuk’s own decision to stop fasting after reported assurances. What was not settled was the resignation demand, the protest coalition’s momentum, or the public argument over how far the state had gone — or should go — in responding to both a grievance movement and a public-order problem. `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`, `[`(#)`

## How we know this

**How this piece is framed:** A tactical stop inside an ongoing protest cycle: the news is the end of Sonam Wangchuk’s hunger strike, but the story’s center of mass is whether anything was actually settled — and the evidence says only partly. Wangchuk appears to have paused one pressure tactic after negotiations and assurances, while the broader movement kept its demands, the state kept combining remedial steps with crowd control, and the core exam-leak controversy remained disputed in detail.
