# writing-ergonomics.md — conveying so the reader can receive

This is the **craft** doctrine: *how* we convey, so the information transfers as
cleanly as possible into the reader's understanding. It is distinct from
[spirit.md](spirit.md), which governs *what* we convey and *whether* we convey it
honestly. Ergonomics serves the faithful transfer of reality — it is **never** a license
to trade accuracy for smoothness.

It is mostly evaluated **where the words are laid down** — it is a drafting posture and a
review lens, not a separate upfront plan. You cannot pre-compute good prose; you can only
write toward this and check against it.

---

## The goal: reading that feels like a download
The reader should not have to **fight** our prose to extract the meaning. Word choice,
phrasing, and ordering should get out of the way, so the information transfers as
directly as possible — as close to a clean download as language allows. Effort the reader
spends decoding *us* is effort stolen from understanding *the world*.

## Build the reader's model
Information has dependencies. Establish a foundation before the thing that rests on it;
introduce a premise before the conclusion that needs it; define a term before you lean on
it. Order details and concepts so each is ready when the next arrives — the reader's
mental model should assemble smoothly, not be asked to hold unresolved pieces in suspense.
Don't make the reader carry more open loops than the material truly requires.

## Tempo
Pace it right: don't dump everything at once, and don't pad. Give weight where weight is
due; move quickly where the ground is simple. Let the rhythm match the structure of the
idea, not a fixed cadence.

## Phrasing and logic
Say it precisely and cleanly. Keep logic chains **no longer than the point warrants** —
and **exactly as long as it does** warrant. Some real ideas require long chains; do not
truncate them to look simple. Keep premise → conclusion threads clear and followable. Cut
the words the reader would otherwise have to work to forgive.

## Write for long legs
We do **not** compromise meaning or accuracy for ease of reading, or for the level of the
reader. (Nietzsche: write for those with long legs.) The meaning stays exactly right. We
do not dumb down, we do not over-simplify into distortion, and we do not hollow out a
true-but-hard idea to make it softer — that is deception wearing the costume of
accessibility (see spirit.md).

Not every piece carries a long-leg requirement — match the demand to the material — but
where the real idea is demanding, we keep it whole.

## Gap-closing, not meaning-lowering
For readers not yet at the level, provide gap-closing elements where it makes sense — a
brief definition for a technical term, a quick orientation, a concrete example — placed
**around** the meaning, never **instead of** it. Use the precise or technical word when it
is the *accurate* word, and supply its meaning rather than swapping in a vaguer one. The
reader who isn't yet at level has resources to close the gap; we offer a hand without
lowering the ceiling.

## Ergonomics never overrides accuracy
If a smoother phrasing would bend the meaning, the meaning wins. If a cleaner ordering
would imply a causation the evidence does not support, the evidence wins. **Clarity that
costs truth is not clarity — it is deception that reads nicely.** Ergonomics serves the
faithful transfer of reality; it is never a license to distort it.
